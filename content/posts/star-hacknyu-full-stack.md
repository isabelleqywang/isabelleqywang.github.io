In fall 2025 I joined the team building the website for HackNYU 2026. I signed up for the frontend. I ended up on both sides of the API, and I think that was the best thing that happened to me that semester.

## Situation

The team had six developers and four designers working in short sprints toward a site that more than 900 attendees would use. The frontend was well staffed. The backend, which had to store and serve past projects and sponsor information, was not.

## Task

First, build interactive animations and responsive UI components in TypeScript from the designers' specs. Then, once the backend gap became obvious, help close it.

## Action

On the frontend I worked closely with the designers to translate their layouts into components that held up across screen sizes, and I paid attention to animation performance so the site felt smooth rather than heavy.

When the backend needed hands, I moved over. I configured the MongoDB database connections and wrote the backend code that stores and serves past projects and sponsor data, which meant learning the data model quickly and agreeing on response shapes with the frontend developers I had just been working alongside.

## Result

The site launched for HackNYU 2026 and served more than 900 attendees, with the projects and sponsors sections backed by the services I built. The switch also made the handoff between frontend and backend smoother, because I knew what both sides needed.

## Looking back

Full stack, but make it situational. I did not plan to become the person who moves between layers, but being willing to do it made me more useful to the team than staying in my lane would have.

The lesson I'm keeping: when you change roles mid project, the first thing to do is understand the contract between the two sides. Everything else follows from that.
